## 3. The asks

Each ask is one item: everything in it is one row on the Asks sheet.

33. **Ask:** Release from the lease once the landlord approves your buyer  
   **Why:** The buyer should owe the rent from then on.  
   **Words to propose:** Upon any assignment approved by Landlord, Tenant and any Guarantor shall be released from all obligations arising after the date of the assignment.  
   **Next step:** Ask that the release start on the day of the approved transfer.  
   Asks sheet, row 15
34. **Ask:** A promise from the landlord's lender to keep your lease  
   **Why:** Without it, a new owner might not keep your lease.  
   **Words to propose:** Tenant's subordination is conditioned on Tenant receiving a non-disturbance agreement from each holder of a mortgage on the Building.  
   **Next step:** Ask whether the building has a mortgage now, and ask for the lender's non-disturbance agreement before you sign.  
   Asks sheet, row 24
35. **Ask:** No rent while the space is unusable, and a way out if repairs drag  
   **Why:** A real lease includes this.  
   **Words to propose:** Rent shall abate while the Premises are untenantable. If the Premises are not restored within [180] days after the casualty, Tenant may terminate this Lease.  
   **Next step:** Ask what insurance the landlord carries to rebuild.  
   Asks sheet, row 23

   Glossary sheet, row 9

   Glossary sheet, row 14
41. **Surrender:** Giving the space back at the end of the lease.  
   Glossary sheet, row 20
42. **Subordination:** Your lease ranks below the landlord's mortgage. If the landlord loses the building, the lender may not have to keep your lease.  
   Glossary sheet, row 22
43. **Force majeure:** Events no one controls, like floods or wars, that can excuse late performance.
